DRAFT

**Complaint/History**
- Patient reports a 3- to 4-day history of itchy, sore, red skin primarily affecting the chest, inner elbows, and hands.
- Symptoms are severe enough to disrupt sleep and concentration at work.
- Describes skin as dry, cracked in some areas, but no bleeding or discharge.
- Symptoms differ from prior eczema episodes, which were limited to the arms.
- Over-the-counter steroid cream (referred to as "sterile cream") and antihistamines tried without relief.
- No recent changes in shower gel, clothing, or known exposure to sick contacts; no fever, respiratory, gastrointestinal, or urinary symptoms.

**Relevant Background**
- Past history of eczema, previously managed with unspecified cream.
- Past history of asthma, not currently active.
- No regular medications.
- No known allergies.
- Works as a project manager in a pharmaceutical company.
- Lives with flatmates; non-smoker; consumes alcohol occasionally (a few beers per week).

**Findings**
- Rash described as red, itchy, sore, with dry and cracked skin.
- Distribution: chest, inner elbows, hands. Not present on face.
- No bleeding, pus, or systemic symptoms.
- Clinician notes rash appearance is consistent with eczema flare based on description, though sound quality limited full assessment.

**Clinician's Assessment**
- Likely flare-up of eczema, possibly exacerbated by environmental or irritant triggers.
- Differential not fully explored due to remote consultation and poor audio quality.

**Plan/Follow-up**
- Prescribe stronger topical steroid cream.
- Prescribe emollient ("ammolians") for use in bath/shower to moisturize skin; use regularly for 7–10 days.
- Offer prescription for fexofenadine (stronger antihistamine) to try for itch control.
- Advised to keep trigger diary (e.g., soaps, fabrics, stress).
- Follow up in one week if symptoms do not improve.
- No immediate referral or additional investigations proposed.
