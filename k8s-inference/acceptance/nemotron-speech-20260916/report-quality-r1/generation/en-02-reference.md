DRAFT

**Complaint/History**
- Patient reports a 4-day history of intensely itchy, sore, and red skin.
- Affected areas: chest, hands, arms, and inside the elbows.
- Symptoms are severe enough to disrupt sleep and concentration at work.
- No fever, cough, breathing difficulties, urinary symptoms, or gastrointestinal issues reported.
- Skin is dry and cracked in some areas, but no bleeding or discharge noted.
- Symptoms differ from prior eczema episodes, which were limited to the arms.
- Over-the-counter steroid cream and antihistamines tried recently with no improvement.
- No clear trigger identified; patient denies changes in shower gel, clothing, or known exposures.
- Symptoms interfere with daily activities, including outdoor plans like hiking.

**Relevant Background/Medications/Allergies**
- Past medical history: eczema (previously managed with topical cream and shower product), past asthma (resolved).
- No current regular medications.
- No known allergies.
- Lives with flatmates; works as a project manager at a pharmaceutical company.
- Non-smoker; consumes a few beers per week.

**Findings**
- Skin: red, dry, cracked, intensely itchy; no pus or blood discharge.
- Distribution: chest, hands, arms, inside elbows; not on face.
- No systemic symptoms.

**Clinician's Assessment**
- Possible flare-up of eczema, though more widespread and severe than previous episodes.
- Differential not explicitly discussed, but presentation is consistent with eczematous dermatitis.

**Recorded Plan/Follow-up**
- Prescribe stronger topical steroid.
- Prescribe emollients for use in bath/shower, to be used regularly for 7–10 days.
- Prescribe Fexofenadine (stronger antihistamine).
- Advised to monitor for potential triggers (e.g., soaps, shower gels, fabrics).
- Follow-up advised in one week if symptoms do not improve.
- Patient has no questions and agrees with plan.
